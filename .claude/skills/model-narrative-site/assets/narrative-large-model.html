One file, 1,426 features

The structure came out of a BIM model as plain features: points and planes to stand on, sketches traced from each slab edge, extrudes, Booleans and placements. No meshes are stored. Everything you see is rebuilt from those definitions.

Forty round columns

Each column is a 1,600 mm circle on its own plane at −19.4 m, extruded straight up. The shortest stops after 7.2 m; the longest runs 375.5 m to the eastern roof.

A floor is a sketch

Every slab starts as a closed outline drawn in the plane of its level, traced edge by edge from the source model. A second sketch on the same plane marks the core opening.

Extrude · Boolean difference

One metre down, the core cut out

The outline is extruded 1,000 mm below its level, the opening is extruded the same way, and a Boolean difference subtracts one from the other. That's three features per floor, repeated 129 times.

Where it really stands

The columns were drawn on a local grid. Forty placement features move them onto the survey grid: a 0.978° turn and a shift to E 496,509.976 m, N 2,492,062.527 m. The fine grid is the survey grid; the one turning away from it is the local grid.

Those coordinates are too large for the 32-bit numbers a GPU draws with, so this page moves the survey origin to the tower before the model is built. The shapes don't change.
